In this comic, the Daytona Speedway is compared with various particle accelerators, even though the two are totally separate things[citation needed].

"eV" stands for "electron Volt", a non-SI unit of energy. It's typically used inparticle physics, where exceedingly small & light particles are accelerated to exceedingly high velocities approaching the speed of light. Trying to increase the resulting energy (E=(γmvc)2+(mc2)2−mc2≈12mv2, wherevis velocity) leads to ever larger machines, having circumferences of dozens of kilometers, necessary because of the centrifugal forces at those speeds. This also makes them far more expensive. Judging by collision energy alone, a racecar is a far cheaper way to cause a collision than a particle accelerator (and it has a shorter track).

In a cyclotron, introducing antimatter circling in the opposite direction would allow more energy to be put into the collision products because the original particles are destroyed (at least in theory; withbaryons, it is often the case that only some of thequarks/antiquarks annihilate), so the equivalent energy of their rest mass—and, more significantly, their remaining kinetic energy after the collision—does not detract from the energy budget available to create collision products. This does not matter as much as people think it does, but it would lead to audience excitement because of pure energy, i.e. light, being emitted.

Introducing a hypothetical antimatter race car going in the wrong direction thus not only results in even more carnage but in spectacular fireworks as well. The fact that you, as the spectator in the stands, would not survive such an energy blast (In Hiroshima only a few grams of matter were actually converted to pure energy) is typical of XKCD.
