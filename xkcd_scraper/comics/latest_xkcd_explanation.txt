TheJupiter Icy Moons Explorer(JUICE) is a scientific space exploration spacecraft created by theEuropean Space Agency(ESA).  Its planned voyage began in 2023 and includesflybysofthe Moon(2024),Earth(2024),Venus(2025), Earth (2026), and Earth (2029), before orbitingJupiter(2031) and finally settling into an orbit aroundGanymedein 2034. The many flybys it will make providegravity assists(25 in total) to help it reach its final destination. Gravity assists work by using the celestial bodies' orbital and gravitational energy (instead of rocket fuel) to accelerate JUICE high enough up through the Sun's gravity well to settle into Jupiter's gravity well, therefore reducing the amount of rocket fuel needed to reach its mission destination as well as the mission costs. Each such flyby transfers momentum from the celestial body to the probe, allowing it to accelerate outwards in the solar system using less propellant than if it were to simply use some form ofNewtonian propulsion. Momentum is conserved; the celestial bodies lose exactly the same amount of momentum as the probe gains. However, the probe is so much less massive than the celestial bodies that the effect on those bodies isnegligible.
