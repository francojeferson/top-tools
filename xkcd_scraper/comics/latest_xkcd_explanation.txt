This is a spin on a classic lecture demonstration of the conservation of angular momentum, whereby someone holding dumbbells with arms extended on a slowly rotating swivel chair brings them closer in to their body, and (by conservation ofangular momentum) rotates faster. The expectation is that they then hold the dumbbells further out again to slow down.

In this comic,Beret Guyperforms the demonstration accordingly besideCueballandHairbun. However, he eventually spins up fast enough to exceed his breakup rotation rate, as an astronomical object would. An example of this is a star being formed out of a collapsing spherical cloud of gas. In such astrophysical situations, exciting things happen to that spherical cloud, such as not being a sphere any more (these things tend to form disks instead). Another feature of such accretion disks is that the central protostar emitsastrophysical jetsalong the axis of rotation, which carry angular momentum away and permit the object to continue rotating close to its breakup velocity instead of disintegrating by rotating too fast to stay gravitationally bound. The particles in these jets are very fast-moving, which means both that they're hot and that they have a lot of momentum themselves. This is most likely one of thestrange powersof Beret Guy. The upward-going jet pierces the ceiling, causing chunks of it to fall, and the downward-going one both sets the floor on fire and also, presumably via conservation of (linear) momentum, propels Beret Guy upwards. Though the downward-going jet should impart only the same, but opposite, momentum as the upward-going one, the more immediate interaction with and rebound from the the floor may be the prime reason that it propels Beret Guy preferentially upwards against gravity.

TheHertzsprung–Russell diagramshows the relationship between the stars' absolute magnitudes or luminosities and their effective temperatures (colors). The title text suggests there is a (fictional) Hertzsprung–Russellacademicdiagram showing the relationship between professors' effective temperature and their rating on theRate My Professorssite. What it would mean to 'do well' on this diagram is unclear, since the two factors have little relationship to start with. In the real world, most professors would not be meaningfully differentiated in their temperature, except perhaps in the metaphorical sense ("My professor is so hot"). However, the axial jets shown in the comic imply a much higher effective temperature, especially given the flames visible below Beret Guy's chair.
