The comic shows the scenario of an asteroidon a collision coursewith the Earth, and the crewed space mission sent to prevent this, as as in the filmsArmageddon,Deep Impactand various other storiesnotdating from 1998. Typically, such fictional missions are sent to blow the offending objects up (or else the more realistic and practical option of ensuring that they are nudged away from the Earth-hitting course), but here they are apparently attempting to gain access to the asteroid electronically and implement a "noclip" command.

This refers tonoclip mode in video games: "A cheat that allows players to pass through normally impenetrable objects – walls, ceilings, and floors – by disabling clipping." In most multiplayer games this would be considered cheating and result in a ban. In others it may disable achievements, or requires explicitly and visibly enabling cheats. Often it functions from a built-in developer console, such as inSourcegames such asHalf-lifeorPortal, where it was originally made available to shortcut some aspects of gameplay testing that was left in either as anEaster Eggor just by being forgotten. In other cases, though, it requires a third-party hacked client, such as inGeometry Dash, where the noclipmodin many clients allows the player to pass through obstacles without dying. By enabling noclip on an asteroid headed for Earth, the asteroid will no longer interact with Earth, thus saving it. The only issue, however, is that the astronauts can't get to the ship if they can't push against the asteroid! This error would be trivial if they were tethered to the ship or possess personal propulsion units to use once they are no longer able to stand on the asteroid, but the mission does not seem to have enough foresight to include either of these things.
