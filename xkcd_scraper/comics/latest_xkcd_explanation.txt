This comic imagines what would happen ifelevatorswere named literally, and hence could only elevate themselves and where unable to descend, resulting in them being single use, like a rocket going to space. It shows the elevators being ejected out of the top of the building, and then crashing into a heap on the ground, which means that a new elevator must be purchased and put in place every time someone in the building wants to go up. In atypical modern elevator, the cars go up and down for the entire life of the elevator system, which can be decades. Re-use is not an issue because a real-world elevator car is normally suspended from cables attached to its top or a hydraulic cylinder at the bottom.

Some early lift designs did come somewhat closer to the concept of the disposable car elevator.  APaternoster liftuses a continuous chain of elevator cars moving at a slow fixed speed as an alternative to the single car design. A Paternoster avoids the need for a door that opens and closes, any form of floor alignment or the car calling and control system, all of which required either an expensive (for the time) relay based responsive system or an attendant riding in the elevator. Paternoster lifts were popular when the cost of single car control systems outweighed those of a chain of cars and the accessibility/safety issues of passengers boarding them in motion.

One concept for a disposable lift elevator could be conceptually similar to a single direction paternoster where a car is lifted a fixed height (ie one "floor") and then held in place while a new car is installed below it. When that car is raised to the same height it pushes the first car up another floor. This creates a single upwardly moving stack of cars with the topmost car being ejected out of the shoot when it reaches the top floor. This could be justified under certain demand scenarios where there will always be a load ready for the next car and a 1 floor lift mechanism is the only option available. This system would be similar to how construction cranes self assemble or the construction technique ofClimbing formwork. Still, even under this highly optimistic scenario, the simple cars could likely be returned for reuse via an out of band process like a slide or zipline.

The comic is probably a parodic commentary on the difference between expendable spacecraft, which for a long time were the only real means to access space, andreusable spacecraft, which are only fairly recently being developed in earnest, having significant or (potentially) full reusability aside from the fuel load and any cargo. Aspace elevatorshould be even more like the 'non-disposable' travel system that this comic's characters apparently hope for, but no such reference is directly made.

The title text notes that another advantage of elevators that go down as well as up is that people don't need to find another means to descend from the upper floors of the building. Apparently, rather than make the lengthy walk down the stairs, people had been sliding down them, which was presumably faster and less tiring. However, it necessitated the use of shields (which they presumably had to take up with them in the elevator) to prevent injuries from friction burns and blows due to the uncontrolled descent. This parodies the use of shields in space travel to protect against heating duringre-entryand impacts on landing.
