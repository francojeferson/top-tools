This is the fifth comic in theStargazingseries, following3072: Stargazing 4, which came out one year and five months before.

The comic continues the series withMeganas the host of a stargazing tour, giving a mix of accurate astronomical information and information that is either nonsensical or trivial.

In the first panel, the host explains that her efforts to share "cool space facts" have been covered up by other astronomers (she also referred to her often inaccurate information this way in Stargazing 4). In this case it turns out she has literally been trapped under a tarp, but escaped by chewing through it. It is unclear if or why this was the most practical method of escape.

In the second panel, she suggests that the viewers "spy on some stars". While this choice of words is uncommon in astronomy circles, it is partly due to her belief that stars are birds of the sky. This is inaccurate in many ways: stars are larger than birds, they are further away, and they are not animate. More pertinently, though, birds already occupy the sky, so they are already 'the birds of the sky'. She also suggests taking binoculars frombird-watchersif one doesn't have a set, which may anger the bird-watcher, as well as being a criminal offence.

She then talks about rippling colours in the sky, referring toaurorae, comparing them toscreensaversand requesting people not to move their mouse and cause the screensaver to disappear. This is only possible if the sky was a computer screen in a simulation. Screensavers are usually simple computer generated animations (such as changing colours or slowly moving images) that play when a computer is inactive and are considered nice to look at. They were originally to preventscreen burn-in, which is not an issue that would affect the sky. Megan's comment about moving the mouse refers to moving the cursor to reawaken the computer. This is impossible to do in the sky.[citation needed]The idea of the sky as a screen has been referenced before in395: Morning.

In the last panel, she refers to the now-disproved theories thatcometspredict doom, in the process calling herself someone who signals the coming of astronomy and "cool facts".

In the title text, theSunandMoonare compared. The Sun is actually about 400 times larger than the Moon (in diameter; about 64 000 000 times in volume), which means that the statement that it is over twice as large is true, but massively understates the comparison.
