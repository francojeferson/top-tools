The phrase “couldn't be elected dogcatcher” refers to an incredibly unpopular person, anddates from the late 19th century.Ponytail, perhaps a journalist, says that the news they've uncovered about someone would make them that unpopular. Adogcatcher's job—that is, an animal control officer's job—includes trapping and impounding stray dogs. But Cueball believes that the use of the metaphor is offensive to animal control officers, even though the topic is not about animal control. In the USA, many city officials are elected, not appointed, and even though dog catchers are not elected, the notion continues that dogcatcher is the city office with the lowest prestige. In recent times, the only elected dog catcher was Zeb Towne, who waselected dog catcher in Duxbury, Vermontbetween about 2003 and 2018.

“Catching strays”, a metaphor inspired by the act of being hit by stray bullets, refers to someonereceiving insults or criticism when they weren’t the subject of the conversation. Megan then makes a pun—the main point of the comic—on “catching strays” as in rounding up stray dogs and “catching strays” as in being hit by stray bullets.

The title text seems like an accidental repetition at first glance, but is actually using the same phrase both literally and metaphorically. It could be paraphrased as either “People out here, trapping feral animals, are being randomly insulted” or “People out here, being randomly insulted, are trapping feral animals”. The insults against them being either because ofordespite the practical work done to capture animals, depending upon emphasis and perhaps the wider context.
