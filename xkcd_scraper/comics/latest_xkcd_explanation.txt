Tectonic platescome from the titular theory that explains why sections of the Earth'slithospheremove, which is the rocky terrain that covers the planet. Some of its driving forces are defined to identity how these tectonic plates move. In this comic, someone (probably a geologist) describes three of them to an audience, but only the first two are genuine:

As presented in the comic,tauntingseems to be the act of dangling something to tempt the plate to lunge upwards in an attempt to grab it, as if it were a cat chasing a toy. This creates areverse faultthat allows them to study the layers of geology it exposes. Geological faults do not really form in this way, since the Earth's crust has no motivation to chase after any kind of bait.

In reality, this would be extremely dangerous, with the force of the rising fault behaving not unlike an earthquake. The characters in the comic seem unbothered by this. It's possible that there has been some miscalculation by them, since the dip of the fault (the angle from horizontal) appears to be about 60°, but a thrust fault (which they supposedly have set their taunting device for) should have a dip of less than 45°. The dip angle is what distinguishes a reverse fault from a thrust fault.

The title text lists a further danger. It advises not to try this over amantlehotspot, as it would presumably result in avolcanic eruptionthat spews molten lava everywhere.
