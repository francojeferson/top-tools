Siege engineswere large constructions often brought to a location, or built from scratch, to help attack fortified positions such as castles during a military operation known as asiege. They would be deployed at some distance from the target, beyond the defender's range, and then either be moved closer to the defended position or use their in-built long-range capability to bombard the defenders. The ones moved closer would include those that were intended to make contact with the castle, such as battering rams or siege towers, and would be equipped with protective roofs/walls to let the attackers use themrelativelysafely within the range of the defenders' own weaponry, in order to either climb/breach the walls or force open a sealed entrance.

For whatever reason, in this particular case the 'standard' siege engines have not helped, in what may have been a medieval siege against a medieval castle. In a break from the historic theme, the besiegers have turned to the designers of theChernobyl New Safe Confinementto solve their problems. This might help in several possible ways. Some of the possible aims of a siege are to prevent the besieged from leaving and for any reinforcing troops or supplies to be provided, in a process known asinvestment. The containment structure would effectively serve to physically block these accesses. Alternatively, in creating a siege-structure many times larger than the besieged one, it may give them a far greater set of options for bombarding or invading the target. On top of that, at the very least, the act of being encased would isolate those inside from daylight and weather: the lack of natural daylight could be used to confuse or psychologically influence the defenders; if they are relying upon rain for enough drinking water for the long term then they're going to be thirsty. Just the possibility that the besieging force iscapableof ordering up such a large structure might also be enough to make them think twice about the wisdom of trying to turtle-down in a castle.

The real-life structure in use at Chernobyl had to be assembled some distance from the reactor, then moved over the reactor, as construction near the final location would have involved too great a degree of exposure to radiation from the site. The assembled structure was then moved along prepared tracks to cover the reactor in a much shorter exposure window for the construction crew, effectively achieving the goal of containment while minimising radiation damage to the workers. The method of motion used for this involved using hydraulic cylinders to slide the structure along greased pads, as wheels were not considered possible to use under such a massive construction. The comic version seems to be using either manyself-propelled modular transportersor perhaps some log-based medieval equivalent that might require moving rollers to the front (though there's no sign of anybody doing this, unless they're intentionally keeping hidden from the defences of the fortress). The Chernobyl structure was also given a rather complex shape of doors that hugged the superstructure of the original buildings that it would have to pass over, while the comic's version has a far simpler, but possibly far more imposing, doorway.

Due to its extreme radioactivity, being near the Elephant's Foot can result in a lethal radiation dose in minutes. In addition to the offensive and/or psychological pressures of the containing structure, actuallyaddinga large lethal radioactive mass to the inside of the siege-container would cause even more problems for the defenders they have now trapped in its vicinity.
