Gravitational waveswere predicted bygeneral relativitymany years before scientists were able to directly observe them. Gravitational waves large enough to be found by current detectors can only be created by very heavy objects likeneutron starsorblack holesmoving very quickly, such as when two of them spiral in to each other and merge as shown in the comic. Oscillations from such an event createsine waves, which are smooth.

The French mathematicianJoseph Fourierfamouslyprovedthat sine waves of various amplitudes, frequencies, and phases can be summed to approximate any periodic function, includingsquare waves. Summing sine waves to get a square wave requires sine waves that oscillate faster and faster at lower and lower amplitudes; this is achieved in the comic with more and more, but smaller and smaller, pairs of merging black holes/neutron stars. The square gravitational wave would causespacetimeto oscillate between the the "tall" and "wide" states in sharply changing zones as the gravitational wave passes, as depicted by the series ofrectangular cuboids, as opposed to the equivalent sine-wave form which would produce a rather different effective 3D shape. Square wave gravitational waves would be very unlikely to be found naturally, due to the requirement that multiple independent waves interact in exactly the right way, so observing such waves would greatly surprise scientists. In addition, the gravitational waves from the objects are emitted three-dimensionally, so the requirement that they combine in a very precise way means that observers in most other locations wouldn't see the result as a square wave, but one with differently timed (and possibly suppressed) harmonics according to what angle away from the 'tuned' square-wave direction; it only works if the merging objects are lined up, and even then, only in the direction they're lined up in. Or, if very precise positions/timings are maintained, something very similar to the square wave may also propagate in the reverse direction.

However, the amount of energy required to manipulate black holes or neutron stars in the way illustrated in the comic is astronomically large, as they usually have masses larger than the Sun. Doing so in a manner to produce a gravitational wave with the level of accuracy to functionally work as a square wave would require energy many tens of orders of magnitude larger than humanity has ever produced. In addition, creating aperfectsquare wave would require an infinite number of merging stars, which is not possible in our (presumably) finite universe, though increasingly closeapproximations of a square waveare possible with increasing numbers of stars.

The title text implies that the waves could be given enough energy to destroy or otherwise significantly affect the Earth.
