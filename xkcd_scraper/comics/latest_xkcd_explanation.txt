This comic plays on the idea that theheavy watersuggests the existence of "light water" vianucleonreduction. Since heavy water (or possibly, in this illustration, onlysemiheavy water) hashydrogenatomsthat contains two nucleons andregular waterhas a hydrogen atom that contains one nucleon, an extrapolation could be made to conclude that a new water variant exists calledlight water, which has one or both "hydrogen" atoms that don't contain any nucleons at all. A hydrogen isotope of two nucleons isdeuterium, while a hydrogen isotope of one nucleon is calledprotium. A "hydrogen" atom of zero nucleons would be identical to anium, a fictional isotope as shown in2719: Hydrogen Isotopes. There also technically existspositronium, an 'atom' with a positron assuming a role equivalent to a hydrogen's proton, although this is not an option indicated by the comic.

"Light water", as depicted in this comic, is fictional, as a hydrogen isotope of zero nucleons cannot exist. This is due to the fact that an atom's element type is dependent on the number ofprotons; a hydrogen atom is an atom containing one proton, and all atoms, by definition, have to contain at least one proton. It's possible for a positively chargedantimuonto capture an electron, forming an "atom" calledmuoniumwhich behaves chemically somewhat like hydrogen, with the differences caused by the antimuon's mass being only about one-ninth of a proton's. The antimuon has a half-life of about 2.2 × 10−6s — long enough to measure some of the properties of "light water" made with it, but not long enough to collect or to do much in the way of chemistry.

A hydrogen atom has an associatedelectron, balancing the positive charge on its proton. The "light water" depicts the electron present, meaning the "light water" molecule would just be ananion, specifically ahydroxideion (if there's still one atom of regular hydrogen), or anoxideion (if both hydrogen atoms are "light").

The title text clarifies that "light" refers tobremsstrahlung. Bremsstrahlung is a type of electromagnetic radiation generated when a high-energy charged particle (usually an electron) passes close to the nucleus of an atom where it is deflected or slowed through Coulomb interactions, losing energy in the form ofphotons. This is therefore a wordplay on "light" (not heavy) and light (photons), as well as referencing the presence of the electron in "light water".

Although not depicted in the comic,deuterium-depleted wateris sometimes known as light water. The termlight-water reactoralso shares a similar name.Tritiated wateris another isotopic form of water not depicted in the comic, which could be considered 'heavier water'.
