'Living in an ivory tower' is a figure of speech meaning that someone is secluded or doesn't understand people's everyday lives. The phrase is most commonly used to refer to academics whose concerns and understanding are restricted to their own fields of research, which are usually far removed from the cares of ordinary folk. It may also be applied to a 'ruling class' to suggest that they are drawn from a narrow demographic that has no experience of the lives of the majority and cannot understand them.

TheCueballin the tower interprets the complaints of the angry mob about "ivory tower elites" as having this metaphorical sense (despite the fact that he is in a literal tower). He points out that the phrase "ivory tower elite" is just an anti-intellectualism and that they are just mad at academics for their perceived superiority.

However, it transpires that not only is there a literal tower, but it is a literalivorytower, and the crowd are specifically angry that Cueball built a tower out of ivory, causing a collapse in the elephant population. Ivory is a material that comes from tusks, most commonly of elephants, and it is a sought after material, often poached illegally and leading toelephants becoming endangered. To build a tower made of ivory from elephant tusks would likely have required Cueball and his associates to kill a very large number of elephants. Cueball admits that it was a problem but argues that it was worth it to build the tower because it's "so cool".

The title text citesCITES, an international treaty which provides a framework for regulating trade that impacts endangered species, including the ivory trade. Cueball has presumably violated it by harvesting more ivory than is allowed for in a certain period of time while, at the same time, breaking his city building codes by building the tower unsafely or without the required permissions. Ivory is not a building material allowable within the building codes of most municipalities.[actual citation needed]
