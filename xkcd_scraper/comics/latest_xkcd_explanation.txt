CueballandPonytailare discussing the possibility of the newVera C. Rubin Observatorydiscovering an Earth-sized object in the far reaches of theSolar System, as one of several so far unsupported hypothetical candidates as the nextPlanet Nine, orTen, that might be on the cusp of being discovered. It would have to be very distant, however, since gravitational effects of an Earth-sized object in the vicinity of Pluto should already have been detected.

Cueball is intrigued by Ponytail's initial imagery, of a planet similar to Earth but with internal heat to make up for the limited heat it would be able to receive from the Sun. In his mind, this leads straight to the exciting possibility that it might even harbor life. Ponytail, being a geologist, has another objective in mind. She is more excited by the idea that it might haveplate tectonics, which none of the other terrestrial planets have to any significant degree (excluding, perhaps, some of the moons of the gas giants, especially those where there is a possibly mobile ice surface rather than rocky plates such as Earth has).

The title text continues with the geologist's bias towards being interested in rock-based situations (i.e. silicates) by dismissing the presence of water-ice (a possible hint of the presence of liquid water, an assumed prerequisite for life). Though they expect there to be a layer of ice, they would rather that this would somehow go away in order that the 'real surface' could be studied properly. Ironically, based on our current understanding of plate tectonics, the lack of water would prevent "normal" (i.e. earth-like) plate tectonics. Water is needed to lubricate subduction zones, without it oceanic plates could not slide under the continental plates.

This comic may have been inspired by theactual discovery ofan"enormous dwarf planet"by the VCRO, although this is still far from being an Earth-sized planet.
