In the comic,Cueballseems to have been approached by a child who is afraid of being hit by ameteor. Cueball attempts to reassure the child by telling him that meteors are not likely to hit him, but then spoils this attempt to comfort him by explaining what other disasters are much more likely to kill him. This leads to the child being more afraid, not less, an example of anassurance backfire. The caption elaborates that Cueball, or perhapsRandallhimself, does this often and needs to work on his strategies to reassure people. This could be a behavior that Cueball has had before, like in999: Cougars.

The title text goes further. Rather than a relatively more common small meteor hitting just the child — described in a decidedly non-comforting way as "small and potentially deadly meteors hit the ground all the time" — it explains that it is far more likely for a much rarer but much larger meteor to completely wipe out a larger area of population. Again, this likely fails to make the child feel safer or reassured.

It's true that one is extremely unlikely to be hit by a meteor. Since 1800, there's onlyone well-documented caseof this happening, and since somewhere around15 billion people have lived since then, a very rough estimate of the odds is 1 in 15 billion. The odds ofdyingfrom a meteor(ite) are higher, since these include mass-mortality side effects from large meteor impacts or explosions mentioned in the title text, such as theTunguska meteoror themeteor that killed off the non-avian dinosaurs(a xkcd comic kindof related to this). Theseodds have been estimatedfrom a 1 in 75,000 to a 1 in 1.6 million lifetime chance, with nearly all of the risk coming from large-scale events.

According toresearch, about 50 small meteorites land on Earth every day. But since these are distributed around the world, which is very big[citation needed]and has lots of uninhabited areas, the chance of any of them hitting a person is nearly negligible. Two years ago, however, one came close: in 2024, a doorbell camera caught a meteorite landing in the front of a house in Canada, just a couple of minutes after the owner had left the house to walk his dogs. He saved thevideo.
