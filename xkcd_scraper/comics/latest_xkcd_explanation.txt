TheBanach–Tarski paradoxis a theorem in set-theoretic geometry saying that a solid three-dimensional object — in this case, asports ball— can be taken apart into several pieces (usually five) and those pieces can then be reassembled intotwoidentical copies of the original object. The paradox has been proven true for any object at thetheoreticallevel, but is difficult to replicate in real life because it requires the objects to be made of infinite collections of points, rather than a finite number of atoms.
