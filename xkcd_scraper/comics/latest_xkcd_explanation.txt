Voyager 1is a space probe launched in 1977, almost 50 years ago. It and most other space probes use aRadioisotope thermoelectric generatora radioactive power source, as they are the most reliable power source in areas where solar power is infeasible due to the large distances from the sun reducing solar energy flux. Half a century on, Voyager 1's  power output hasfallen considerablysince then. As a result, the scientists at NASA need to turn off certain scientific instruments in order to conserve the limited amount of power.

In the second panel, they debate whether to turn off thePlasma Wave Subsystem(an instrument that measures electron density in interstellar space) or themagnetometer(an instrument that measures magnetic field strength). As of the date of this comic posting (September 2026), this is correct; these are the only twoscientific instruments currently activeon Voyager 1.

In this comic's reality, it's revealed there is something else drawing power: Greg has apparently installed a "LED underlighting" system on Voyager 1, which he refuses to turn off, claiming that it would make humans "look uncool in front of aliens". This is less ridiculous than it would initially appear, as the Voyager probes do in fact carry theVoyager Golden Recordwhich in theory would allow aliens to receive a message from Earth encoded in the audio and data recordings. Also, the provision of LED lights (even the dim red ones that were the only kind available when Voyager 1 was launched) would presumably increase the chance of aliens spotting the probe in the first place.

The title text mentions that Greg has in fact added even more "entertainment" features to Voyager 1, including a "stupid laser show" (lasers do not scatter due to the absence of particles in a vacuum, making an Earth-style laser show very difficult to view in space) and "useless sound system" (assound does not travel in space), which were only turned off after a "huge ordeal" which nobody wants to repeat.
