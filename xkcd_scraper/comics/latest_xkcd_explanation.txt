In Planck units, speeds are measured in terms of the speed of light,c. 0.000000097cis ~104.6 km/h or 65mph.

The "Next Exit" sign references thePlanck Length, which is the (theoretically) smallest possible length, equal to approximately 1.616255 × 10-35m. 1.39 × 1039Planck lengths is about 22.5 km, or 14.0 miles. It would likely be impractical for drivers to count the digits in that sign at highway speeds[citation needed]. The signs would be better (in both readability and ruining the joke) if they provided the numbers in scientific notation for those drivers who understood it, perhaps in addition to the displays as currently shown.

The title text claims that highway engineers like Planck units because the energy capacity of a gas tank is 1. It is true that the Planck Energy is approximately equal to the chemical energy stored in a typical gas tank, seePlanck Energy. From a certain point of view, cars' gas gauges already commonly use 1 to denote a tank's maximum volume: Empty means 0, then come fractions ¼, ½, and ¾, and then Full means 1. Of course, these numbers correspond to different real volume measurements between different makes of car. However, it is unclear why the units used to measure the capacity of individual gas tanks would matter to highway engineers, who are primarily concerned with the safety of the roads those vehicles drive on, not how they operate.
