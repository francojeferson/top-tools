This comic plays on the idea that theheavy watersuggests the existence of "light water" vianucleonreduction. Since heavy water has ahydrogenatomthat contains two nucleons andregular waterhas a hydrogen atom that contains one nucleon, an extrapolation could be made to conclude that a new water variant exists calledlight water, which has a "hydrogen" atom that doesn't contain any nucleons at all. A hydrogen isotope of two nucleons is adeuterium, while a hydrogen isotope of one nucleon is called aprotium. A "hydrogen" atom of zero nucleons would be identical to anium, a fictional isotope as shown in2719: Hydrogen Isotopes.

"Light water", as depicted in this comic, is fictional, as a hydrogen isotope of zero nucleons cannot exist. This is due to the fact that an atom's element type is dependent on the amount ofprotons; a hydrogen atom is, by definition, an atom containing one proton, and all atoms, again by definition, have to contain at least one proton.

A hydrogen atom has an associatedelectron, balancing the positive charge on its proton. The "light water" depicts the electron present, meaning it would just be ahydroxideion (if there's still one atom of regular hydrogen), oroxideion (if both hydrogen atoms are "light").

The title text clarifies that "light" refers tobremsstrahlung. Bremsstrahlung is a type of electromagnetic radiation generated when a high-energy charged particle (usually an electron) passes close to the nucleus of an atom where it is deflected or slowed through Coulomb interactions, losing energy in the form ofphotons. This is therefore a wordplay on "light" (not heavy) and light (photons), as well as referencing the presence of the electron in "light water".

Although not depicted in the comic,deuterium-depleted wateris sometimes known as light water. The termlight-water reactoralso shares a similar name.
