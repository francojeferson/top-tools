'Living in an ivory tower' is a figure of speech meaning that someone is secluded or doesn't understand people's everyday lives. The phrase is most commonly used to refer to academics whose concerns and understanding are restricted to their own fields of research, which are usually far removed from the cares of ordinary folk. It may also be applied to a 'ruling class' to suggest that they are drawn from a narrow demographic that has no experience of the lives of the majority and cannot understand them.

TheCueballin the tower interprets the complaints of the angry mob about "ivory tower elites" as having this metaphorical sense (despite the fact that he is in a literal tower). He points out that the phrase "ivory tower elite" is just an anti-intellectualism and that they are just mad at academics for their self-perceived superiority.

However, it transpires that not only is there a literal tower, but it is a literalivorytower, and the crowd are specifically angry that Cueball built a tower out of ivory, causing a collapse in the elephant population. Ivory is a material that comes from tusks, most commonly of elephants, and it is a sought after material, often poached illegally and leading toelephants becoming endangered. To build a tower made of ivory from elephant tusks would likely have required Cueball and his associates to kill a very large number of elephants.  Cueball admits that it was a problem but argues that it was worth it to build the tower because it's "so cool".

A roughly comparable tower structure, such as a stone-built lighthouse, may have a mass of around 3,250 tonnes, with a material volume of roughly 1,180 cubic metres. If this stone structure was to be built entirely of an equivalent form of ivory blocks instead, this could be around 2,125 tonnes, requiring the material of almost 92,000 average (male, African elephant) tusks, assuming no waste. This requires the deaths (or maybe preferably, but still not ideal, a more humane detusking) of at least 46,000 elephants. As the total number of African elephants (the most common species) isestimated to be around 400,000 worldwide, this would indeed represent a major population decrease.  It would be significantly fewer, but still not an inconsequential number, if it were ivory sheets carefully fashioned to decoratively surface a structure of another material such as stone.

The title text citesCITES(Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora), an international treaty which helps regulate trade that impacts endangered species, including the ivory trade. Cueball has presumably flouted it, which means to ignore a law and carry on anyway, by harvesting or importing ivory illegally, while at the same time breaking his city building codes by building the tower out of ivory. Ivory is not a construction material allowed by the building codes of most municipalities.[actual citation needed]
